Background

How I Got Here (Or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Shell)

Picture this: 2021, first year of college. I'm sitting in my cybersecurity class learning about the CIA triad—Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability. And I'm thinking, "Okay cool, so that's cybersecurity. Just... CIA."

2022, same thing. More CIA triad. I'm starting to wonder, "Wait, is that EVERYTHING in cybersecurity?"

Then I came across TryHackMe and HackTheBox. My brain practically exploded. Turns out there's an entire universe beyond the CIA triad — and I'd been staring at the first page of it.

2023 hits, and I properly dive in. My first room: OSINT. No clue what I'm doing. The room loads, and I get... one picture. Just one. I stare at it. Refresh the page thinking it didn't load properly. Same picture.

Then I find a Medium article explaining the solution. What happens next? Mind blown. This person extracted a user's password. From. A. Picture.

That was it. I was hooked. Didn't stop from that day.

Projects

DefenseSphere

Final Year Project

SIEM-Style Security Monitoring Platform

GitHub

Problem

Small teams need SIEM-style monitoring but most tools are expensive, noisy, and built for enterprise SOCs. I wanted to build something Tier-1 analysts could actually use.

Approach

Built a platform that ingests Windows, Linux and network logs, with correlation logic and alerting use cases for brute-force attempts, unauthorized file changes, and anomalous user behaviour — plus an RBAC admin dashboard mirroring Tier-1 SOC triage workflows.

Impact

Cut simulated mean time to detect by ~40% in red team attack scenarios. Trained a Random Forest phishing detector on 10,000+ email samples (87% accuracy, 25+ features), and added tamper-evident audit logging using hash chaining.
